2. Morphology

2.1. Basic Principles

Words in this language are constructed exclusively through combinations of prefixes, infixes, and suffixes, without the use of roots.

Previously formed words may also be used as new affixes.

2.2. Basic Affix Structures

  • Prefix: [string]+a
  • Infix: [string]+i
  • Suffix: [string]+u

The string within square brackets is optional. Therefore, a, i, and u may each function as standalone affixes.

2.3. Affix Naming Rules

  1. Three or more consecutive consonants are prohibited.
  2. More frequently used affixes should be shorter.
  3. Duplicate affixes are prohibited.

2.4. Semantic Classification of Prefixes

The initial letter of a prefix determines its basic semantic category.

Initial LetterMeaning
nNouns referring to objects or substances
lParts of speech representing abstract concepts
vVerbs

Only these three categories are permitted. No additional categories shall be introduced.

3. Word Formation

3.1. Basic Word Formation

Words are formed by combining multiple affixes. Spaces must not be inserted within a word.

3.2. Recursive Affix Formation

Previously formed words may be used as affixes according to the following structure:

e+[word]+e+[a/i/u]

3.3. Proper Nouns

Proper nouns are formed according to the following structure:

e+[proper noun]+e+[a/i/u]

4. Syntax

4.1. Basic Word Order

The basic word order is VSO (Verb–Subject–Object).

  • V: Verb
  • S: Subject
  • O: Object

4.2. Direct Object Marker

tu is used as the direct object marker.

4.3. Coordination

ku is used as a suffix meaning "and".

4.4. Possession and Association

mu is used as a suffix meaning "of".
